\begin{abstract}
We investigate the security implications of Apple's data memory-dependent prefetcher (DMP), beginning with the M1. Our scope has broadened from reproducing GoFetch to studying DMP exploitation through both Augury and GoFetch. At the midpoint, our experimental work has focused on Augury activation and reverse engineering. We describe our replication methodology, summarize the available timing outputs and their limitations, and outline the remaining exploitation demonstrations. Our planned novel investigation compares selected activation conditions under Asahi Linux on M1 and on an M4 chip, with particular attention to distinguishing changed behavior from evidence of mitigation.
\end{abstract}
